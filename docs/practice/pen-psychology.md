# Pen & Paper Psychology — why kiruk draws first

The studio's claim: **paper + pen is not nostalgia, it's a thinking tool.** This doc collects the _why_ (so it's defensible, teachable, and public-worthy) and the _rules_ (so it's actually practiced).

## Why sketching helps thinking (claims to research + cite)

> Each should get a real citation before it goes public. For now, working hypotheses to test against sources.

- **Lower fidelity → more ideas.** Rough sketches signal "not final," so the brain explores more divergent options before committing (vs. a polished mock that feels done).
- **Externalizing offloads working memory.** Getting the idea onto paper frees cognition to recombine and spot relationships ("distributed cognition").
- **Hand–eye loop aids memory & insight.** The motor act of drawing encodes ideas more durably than typing; "thinking with the hand."
- **Constraint breeds clarity.** No undo, no infinite layers → you decide what matters and commit a line.
- **Ambiguity is generative.** A wobbly line can be read multiple ways, inviting reinterpretation; pixel-perfect closes that door early.

## Rules — when to go to paper before pixels

- **Idea / concept stage → always paper first.** No Figma until there's a scribble (matches Non-Negotiable #7).
- **Stuck or looping in software → stop, draw it.** If you've nudged the same element 5×, the problem is conceptual, not pixel.
- **Big decision → sketch the options.** Drawing 3 versions in 5 minutes beats arguing one in your head.
- **Explaining to others → draw it live.** A shared scribble aligns faster than a deck.

## Practice (turn theory into habit)

- Keep the scribble: photograph/scan into `content/scribbles/` or the artifact's `scribble/` folder.
- Date and caption it (what question it was answering).
- Note in `decision-log.md` when a paper session changed a decision — that's the evidence the method works.

kiruk means _scribble_ — and _crazy_ — in Tamil. For weeks the studio was all
operating system: specs, tokens, a monorepo that could run a lab. Beautiful
engine, nothing you could look at. This is the log where kiruk starts to **show
its face**.

## What changed

The first hero tried to be ink — a fluid WebGL field. It looked like smoke.
Wrong language. kiruk isn't ink that bleeds; it's a **pencil that scribbles**.
So the cursor became a pen: drag anywhere and graphite strokes land on white
paper, grainy, catching the tooth, staying where you put them. Underneath, an
eye waits — draw over it and it surfaces. Scribble first, the watching eye
second. That's the whole thesis in one interaction.

The palette collapsed to **black and white only**. Color is something you add
by hand, on an object, when it earns the right — that contrast is where the
digital and the paper blend. Type went hand-drawn for the voice, clean for the
reading.

## How it was decided

None of this was a vibe. The direction is now real because it went through the
studio's own spec process: a founder-decisions pass, then an OpenSpec change
that rewrote the design tokens (paper, ink, a pencil grayscale ramp, an
asset-only "absurd" color group), relaxed the rules that no longer fit, and
validated clean before a single token shipped. Decisions become truth on
purpose here, not by accident.

## What's next

A real pen-and-paper interface end-to-end — a designer's desk you sit at, hands
and all. And the unglamorous spine: a way to run a client universe from first
scribble to handoff without losing the thread. Less machinery for its own sake,
more things you can actually touch.

Everything starts from a scribble. Now the scribble is the screen.
